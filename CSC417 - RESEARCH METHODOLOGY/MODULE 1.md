# Course: Research Methodology

## Week 1: Definition, Characteristics, and Types of Research

### 1. Definition of Research

Research is the systematic inquiry aimed at the advancement of knowledge. It can be defined in three key ways:

- **Data Analysis:** The systematic gathering of data and information and its analysis for the advancement of knowledge.
    
- **Discovery & Revision:** An investigation or experimentation aimed at discovering and interpreting facts, revising accepted theories/laws in light of new facts, or the practical application of new laws.
    
- **Scientific Inquiry:** A systematic inquiry to describe, predict, and control observed phenomena.
    

### 2. Characteristics of Research

For an investigation to be considered "research," it generally exhibits the following features:

1. **Empirical:** Based on direct observations and experimentation.
    
2. **Systematic:** Follows an orderly and sequential procedure.
    
3. **Controlled:** All variables are kept constant except those being tested/experimented upon.
    
4. **Employs Hypothesis:** Guided by an idea or explanation based on known facts (or reasonable assumptions) that has not yet been proven.
    
5. **Analytical:** Involves critical analysis of data to prevent interpretation errors.
    
6. **Quantitative/Statistical:** Data is often transformed into numerical measures and treated statistically.
    
7. **Logical:** Based on valid procedures and principles.
    
8. **Cyclical:** Research starts with a problem and ends with a problem (often opening doors for further research).
    
9. **Critical:** Exhibits careful and precise judgment.
    
10. **Methodical:** Conducted without bias using systematic methods.
    
11. **Replicability:** Designs and procedures are repeatable to allow for valid, conclusive results.
    

### 3. The Two Main Types of Research

Research is broadly categorized into two primary forms based on utility and goal.

#### A. Pure Research (Basic/Fundamental)

- **Goal:** To generate new human knowledge, uncover facts, or formulate fundamental principles (e.g., "Why do humans sleep?").
    
- **Nature:** Based on strict experimentation, observation, and rigorous standards to preserve objectivity.
    
- **Outcome:** Results are typically published in peer-reviewed journals; primarily concerned with theory formulation.
    

#### B. Applied Research (Action Research)

- **Goal:** Borrows ideas from pure research to solve specific, real-world problems (e.g., improving hospital care, marketing a product).
    
- **Nature:** Ethics remain important, but methods can be more relaxed/flexible to fit time constraints or imperfect sample groups.
    
- **Outcome:** Considered successful if it adds value to the stated goal or solves an immediate problem faced by society/industry.
    

### 4. Further Classifications of Research

Research can be further subdivided into comparative pairs:

#### Generative vs. Exploratory

- **Generative Research:** The "pre-definition" stage. It leads to ideas and helps define the problem (e.g., interviews, field observation). Used when looking for new ideas or features.
    
- **Exploratory Research:** Studies a problem that does not yet have a solution. It is the initial stage that provides a basis for conclusive research (helping to form the research design and sampling methodology).
    

#### Descriptive vs. Analytical

- **Descriptive Research:** Describes the "state of affairs" as it currently exists.
    
    - _Key Feature:_ The researcher has **no control over variables**; they only report what is happening.
        
    - _Methods:_ Surveys, comparative methods, and _Ex post facto_ studies.
        
- **Analytical Research:** Uses facts or information already available. The researcher critically evaluates and analyzes the material to discover causes.
    

#### Quantitative vs. Qualitative

- **Quantitative Research:** Measurement of quantity or amount. Applicable to phenomena that can be measured numerically.
    
- **Qualitative Research:** Concerned with quality or kind (e.g., underlying motives, desires, human behavior).
    
    - _Methods:_ In-depth interviews, word association tests, sentence completion, and attitude/opinion research.
        

#### Conceptual vs. Empirical

- **Conceptual Research:** Related to abstract ideas or theory. Mostly used by philosophers/thinkers to develop new concepts or reinterpret existing ones.
    
- **Empirical Research:** Data-based research relying on experience or observation alone.
    
    - _Key Feature:_ Characterized by the experimenter’s control over variables and deliberate manipulation to study effects.
        
    - _Importance:_ Considered the most powerful support for a given hypothesis.
