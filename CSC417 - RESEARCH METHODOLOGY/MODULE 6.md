## Week 6: Experimental Designs, Results, and Discussions

### 1. Experimental Research Design

**Definition:**

Experimental research design is a framework of rules, protocols, and procedures created to conduct research with a scientific approach using two sets of variables.

- **Method:** The first set of variables acts as a constant (control) to measure the differences in the second set (experimental).
    
- **Best Example:** Quantitative research often utilizes this method.
    
- **Goal:** To gather necessary data for making better research decisions and determining facts.
    

**When to conduct Experimental Research:**

1. **Time Factor:** When time is crucial in establishing a relationship between cause and effect.
    
2. **Invariable Behavior:** When there is a consistent, never-changing behavior between cause and effect.
    
3. **Importance:** When the researcher wishes to understand the specific importance of the cause and effect.
    

### 2. Importance of Experimental Design

Choosing a high-quality design is the foundation of a successful study.

- **Strategic Planning:** It allows the researcher to organize the research and set relevant boundaries _before_ beginning practical experiments.
    
- **Reliability:** It increases the reliability of results and helps avoid inconclusive findings.
    
- **Data Analysis:** It structures the research to lead to easier and more accurate data analysis.
    

### 3. Types of Experimental Research Designs

Based on data collection methods, there are three primary types:

#### A. Pre-experimental Research Design

Used when a group (or groups) is kept under observation after implementing factors of cause and effect. It is often a preliminary step to see if further investigation is needed.

- **Key Feature:** Lacks a control group or randomization.
    
- **Sub-types:**
    
    1. **One-shot Case Study:** A single group is exposed to a treatment and then observed. (No pre-test, no control group).
        
    2. **One-group Pretest-posttest:** A single group is tested before the treatment, exposed to the treatment, and tested again.
        
    3. **Static-group Comparison:** Two groups are compared (one receives treatment, one does not), but they are not randomly assigned.
        

#### B. True Experimental Research Design

The most accurate form of research, relying on statistical analysis to prove/disprove a hypothesis. It provides specific scientific evidence.

- **Key Requirement:** It is the only design that can truly establish a **cause-effect relationship**.
    
- **Mandatory Factors:**
    
    1. **Control & Experimental Groups:** Must have a group that _does not_ receive changes and one that _does_.
        
    2. **Manipulated Variable:** The researcher must control the independent variable.
        
    3. **Randomization:** Participants must be randomly distributed to ensure unbiased results.
        

#### C. Quasi-experimental Research Design

The word "Quasi" means "resembling" or "similar."

- **Key Difference:** Similar to True Experimental design, **EXCEPT** participants are **not randomly assigned** to groups.
    
- **Usage:** Used in field settings (like schools or hospitals) where random assignment is irrelevant, unethical, or impossible.
    

### 4. Advantages of Experimental Research

- **Control:** Researchers have firm control over variables.
    
- **Specific Results:** The findings are specific and conclusive.
    
- **Replicability:** Anyone can implement the research; findings can be repurposed for similar ideas.
    
- **Causality:** Clearly identifies the cause-and-effect relationship.
    
- **Foundation:** Acts as an ideal starting point for building new research ideas.
    

### 5. Mistakes to Avoid (Design Checklist)

Any of these errors can compromise the quality of the research:

1. **Invalid Theoretical Framework:** Failing to check if the hypothesis is logical or missing basic assumptions/postulates.
    
2. **Inadequate Literature Study:** Failing to identify knowledge gaps or stating how the research contributes to the field.
    
3. **Insufficient/Incorrect Statistical Analysis:** Using the wrong statistics affects the validity of quantitative evidence.
    
4. **Undefined Research Problem:** The problem statement must be clear to address core issues.
    
5. **Ignoring Research Limitations:** Every study has limitations; these must be anticipated and stated in the conclusion.
    
6. **Ethical Implications:** Failing to minimize risk for participants or address ethical norms questions the validity of the study.
    

### 6. Example of Experimental Design

**The Plant Experiment:**

- **Setup:** A researcher gathers plant samples.
    
- **Randomization:** Half are assigned to sunlight (Experimental Group), half to a dark box (Control Group).
    
- **Control:** All other variables (water, soil, nutrients) are kept constant.
    
- **Result:** Biochemical tests compare outcomes.
    
- **Conclusion:** Any changes are confirmed to be caused by **sunlight**, not other factors.
    

_Note: Experimental research is resource-intensive (time, money) but provides the most conclusive results in scientific approaches._
