## Week 3: Literature Review and Formulation of Research Objectives

### 1. Literature Review

#### Definition

A **Literature Review** is a comprehensive review and analysis of published literature (books, scholarly articles, theses) that relates to a particular research topic or question.

- It acts as a pillar for your research idea, providing **context, relevance, and background**.
    
- **Purpose:** To gain an in-depth understanding of existing research, theories, and ideas in a specific area of study.
    

#### How to Write a Literature Review (The Process)

**Step 1: Search for Relevant Literature**

Find materials that relate to your research question using sources such as:

- **Google Scholar**
    
- **University Online Library**
    
- **Snowballing:** The technique of using the reference list of a relevant paper to find more papers.
    
- **Related Projects or Theses**
    

**Step 2: Evaluate Your Sources**

Ensure sources are credible and fit for purpose. Ask yourself:

- Is the source trustworthy?
    
- Is it relevant to my specific field?
    
- What are the key terms and concepts defined here?
    
- Are there any obvious gaps for further study?
    

**Step 3: Log and Synthesize**

- Use reference managers (e.g., **Mendeley**, **EndNote**) to catalogue information.
    
- Synthesize the information rather than just listing it.
    

**Step 4: Determine Themes, Debates, and Gaps**

- A literature review must be a **critical analysis**, not just a summary.
    
- Identify recurring themes, areas of debate between scholars, and gaps where information is missing.
    

**Step 5: Outline the Structure**

You can structure the review **Chronologically** (by time) or **Thematically** (by topic). Regardless of structure, you must include:

1. A description of the publication.
    
2. A summary of the main points.
    
3. An evaluation of its contribution to the topic.
    
4. Identification of potential gaps.
    

#### Functions of a Literature Review

1. **Demonstrate Understanding:** Shows you have mastered the current research around your question.
    
2. **Establish a Gap:** Proves there is a clear need for your specific research.
    
3. **Build a Conceptual Framework:** Helps you select theories and previous research to build a model that will be tested/validated.
    
4. **Foundation of Methodology:** Helps determine the best methods to use based on what worked (or didn't) in previous studies.
    

### 2. Formulation of Research Objectives

Research objectives specify **how** the research question will be answered and determine **what** will be measured to test the hypothesis.

#### Steps to Formulate Objectives

1. **Identify** the research problem.
    
2. **Review** past studies similar to your problem (look at their methods and variables).
    
3. **Identify Gaps** in the current study (Theoretical, Methodological, or Conceptual gaps).
    
4. **Define** the research question(s) based on these gaps.
    
5. **Revise/Relate** the research problem to the questions/gaps to confirm the actual need for the study.
    
6. **Write** the General and Specific objectives.
    
7. **Incorporate** the objectives into the study.
    

#### Research Aims vs. Research Objectives

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|**Feature**|**Research Aims**|**Research Objectives**|
|**Focus**|Reflects **broad goals** and general direction.|Focuses on **specific actions** to be taken.|
|**Nature**|Not specific; defines the focus or intent.|Practical, specific, and **actionable**.|
|**Example**|"This research aims to **evaluate the performance** of machine learning models in predicting lung disease."|"To **design and implement** a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model for lung disease prediction."|

#### Research Questions vs. Research Objectives

|                                                                               |                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Research Questions**                                                        | **Research Objectives**                                                               |
| **Broad Statement:** Guides the overall direction of the research.            | **Specific Goals:** Measurable targets the research aims to achieve.                  |
| **Identify Problem:** Highlights the main issue to be solved.                 | **Define Outcomes:** States the specific results the study will produce.              |
| **Generative:** Used to generate hypotheses or identify knowledge gaps.       | **Targets:** Used to establish clear, achievable targets.                             |
| **Example:** "How do employees perceive digital transformation in retail HR?" | **Example:** "To assess employee perceptions of digital transformation in retail HR." |
