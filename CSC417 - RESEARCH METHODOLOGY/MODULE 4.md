## Week 4: Features of a Good Research Design

### 1. Definition of Research Design

**Research Design** is the comprehensive structure or "blueprint" of research methods and techniques selected to conduct a study.

- It refines the methods suited to the subject matter and ensures a successful setup.
    
- Defining the topic clarifies the **type** (e.g., experimental, survey, correlational) and **sub-type** (e.g., descriptive case-study) of research.
    

#### Three Main Aspects of Design

Research design often focuses on three key frameworks:

1. **Data Collection Design:** How information will be gathered.
    
2. **Measurement Design:** How variables will be measured.
    
3. **Data Analysis Design:** How the data will be processed and interpreted.
    

### 2. Elements of Research Design

Impactful research minimizes bias and enhances data accuracy. Designs with minimal error margins are ideal. Key elements include:

- **Accurate Purpose Statement:** Clearly defined goals.
    
- **Techniques:** Procedures for data collection and analysis.
    
- **Methodology:** The specific type of research method used.
    
- **Objections:** Anticipating probable objections or limitations.
    
- **Settings:** Where the research takes place.
    
- **Timeline:** The schedule for the study.
    
- **Measurement:** How analysis will be measured.
    

### 3. Characteristics of a Good Research Design

To ensure validity, reliability, and success, a design must possess four key characteristics:

1. **Reliability (Consistency):**
    
    - The design ensures results are accurate and **replicable**.
        
    - _Meaning:_ If the research is conducted again under the same conditions, it should yield similar results.
        
2. **Validity (Accuracy):**
    
    - Uses appropriate measuring tools to gauge results according to objectives.
        
    - _Meaning:_ The data collected and conclusions drawn accurately reflect the phenomenon being studied (it measures what it is supposed to measure).
        
3. **Neutrality (Unbiased):**
    
    - The design ensures assumptions are free from bias.
        
    - _Meaning:_ Projected results and data collection are objective, not influenced by the researcher's personal opinions.
        
4. **Generalization (Applicability):**
    
    - Outcomes should apply to the larger population, not just the restricted sample.
        
    - _Meaning:_ The survey or study results can be applied to any part of the population with similar accuracy.
        

### 4. Classification: Quantitative vs. Qualitative

#### Quantitative Research Design

- **Focus:** Essential when statistical conclusions and actionable numerical insights are needed.
    
- **Usage:** Crucial for organizational growth; uses complex numerical data to guide critical decisions.
    

#### Qualitative Research Design

- **Focus:** Identifies relationships between data and observations to answer the **"why"** and **"how"** of a phenomenon.
    
- **Usage:** Explores respondent perspectives and underlying motives.
    
- _(Note: Unlike quantitative research, qualitative research deals with non-numerical data and does not typically use statistical calculations for validation.)_
    

#### Comparison Table

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|**Aspect**|**Qualitative Research**|**Quantitative Research**|
|**Data Type**|**Non-numerical:** Words, images, sounds.|**Numerical:** Data that can be measured/counted.|
|**Purpose**|To understand concepts, thoughts, or experiences.|To test hypotheses, identify patterns, and make predictions.|
|**Data Collection**|Interviews (open-ended), observations described in words, literature reviews.|Surveys (closed-ended), experiments, observations recorded as numbers.|
|**Data Analysis**|Analyzed by grouping data into **themes** (Thematic Analysis).|Analyzed using **statistical methods**.|
|**Outcome**|Rich, detailed descriptions; uncovers new insights/meanings.|Objective, empirical data; precise measurements.|

### 5. Specific Types of Research Design

Research design is often broken down into these five categories:

#### A. Descriptive Research

- **Goal:** To describe the "state of affairs" or detail a situation/case.
    
- **Focus:** Solely interested in describing **what** is happening, not why.
    
- **Method:** Theory-based; gathering, analyzing, and presenting data.
    
- _Example:_ A census describing the demographics of a city.
    

#### B. Experimental Research Design

- **Goal:** To establish a **cause-and-effect** relationship.
    
- **Focus:** Observes the impact of an **Independent Variable** (cause) on a **Dependent Variable** (effect).
    
- **Method:** Manipulating one variable to see the change in another.
    
- _Example:_ Testing if a new drug (independent) lowers blood pressure (dependent) compared to a placebo.
    

#### C. Correlational Research Design

- **Goal:** To establish a relationship between two closely connected variables.
    
- **Focus:** Non-experimental; determines strength and direction of relationship (Correlation Coefficient: -1 to +1).
    
- **Method:** No assumptions/manipulation; simply measuring two groups/variables.
    
- _Example:_ Studying the relationship between hours spent studying and exam scores (Positive correlation).
    

#### D. Diagnostic Research Design

- **Goal:** To identify the **underlying causes** of specific issues or troublesome situations.
    
- **Structure:** Has three parts:
    
    1. Inception of the issue.
        
    2. Diagnosis of the issue.
        
    3. Solution for the issue.
        
- _Example:_ A company investigating why sales suddenly dropped in a specific region.
    

#### E. Explanatory Research Design

- **Goal:** To explain unexplored aspects of a subject.
    
- **Focus:** Builds on ideas to explore theories further; addresses the **what, how, and why**.
    
- _Example:_ Researching _why_ a specific marketing trend is becoming popular among teenagers.
    

### 6. Benefits of a Good Research Design

1. **Minimizes Errors:** Reduces inaccuracy and keeps the team on the right track.
    
2. **Efficiency:** Ensures efficient use of time and resources (money/personnel).
    
3. **Provides Direction:** Prevents deviation from tasks; identifies major/minor tasks.
    
4. **Ensures Validity/Reliability:** Enhances the legitimacy of findings and allows for replication.
    
5. **Facilitates Problem-Solving:** Helps frame objectives to find the best solution.
    
6. **Better Documentation:** Assists in tracking activities throughout the project.
