## Week 5: Quantitative & Qualitative Approaches and Data Analysis Tools

### 1. Research Approaches Overview

In fields like business, science, and technology, research is conducted using two standard approaches:

- **Qualitative Research:** Relies on verbal and written data (words). Useful for uncovering trends, ideas, and opinions.
    
- **Quantitative Research:** Relies on statistical and logical observations (numbers/graphs). Useful for understanding underlying opinions, motivations, and testing hypotheses.
    

### 2. Definitions

#### Qualitative Research

- **Purpose:** To gain an understanding of human behavior, intentions, attitudes, and experiences based on observation and interpretation.
    
- **Nature:** Unstructured and exploratory; deals with highly complex phenomena.
    
- **Method:** In-depth understanding through interviews with open-ended questions and observations described in words.
    
- **Examples of Data:** Taste, smell, color, texture, transcriptions of interviews.
    

#### Quantitative Research (Empirical Research)

- **Purpose:** To develop hard facts and numerical data to establish **cause-and-effect** relationships between variables.
    
- **Nature:** Relies on methods of natural sciences; results are accurately and precisely measured.
    
- **Method:** Uses surveys, experiments, and computational methods to establish generalized facts.
    
- **Examples of Data:** Age, salary, height, shoe size, test scores.
    

### 3. Differences: Qualitative vs. Quantitative

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|**Feature**|**Qualitative Research**|**Quantitative Research**|
|**Focus**|Understanding human behavior, personalities, and social sciences.|Generating numerical data using logical, statistical, and mathematical techniques.|
|**Approach**|**Subjective** approach.|**Objective** approach.|
|**Expression**|Expressed using **words**.|Expressed using **graphs and numbers**.|
|**Questions**|Open-ended questions.|Multiple-choice questions.|
|**Sample Size**|Requires **few** respondents.|Requires **many** respondents.|
|**Data Collection**|Interviews, focus groups, ethnography, literature reviews.|Experiments, surveys, observations expressed in numbers.|
|**Nature**|**Holistic** in nature.|**Particularistic** in nature.|
|**Reasoning**|**Inductive** reasoning (moving from specific to general).|**Deductive** reasoning (moving from general to specific).|
|**Orientation**|**Process-oriented** inquiry.|**Result-oriented** (does not involve process-oriented inquiry).|
|**Goal**|Develops initial understanding; engages and discovers ideas.|Recommends a final course of action; examines cause and effect.|
|**Research Type**|**Exploratory** research.|**Conclusive** research.|

### 4. Types of Research (Assignment Expanded)

#### A. Types of Quantitative Research

1. **Descriptive Research:** Describes the current status of an identified variable. It provides a systematic overview of the phenomenon (e.g., "What is the average age of the population?").
    
2. **Experimental Research:** A controlled study where the researcher manipulates one variable (independent) to determine its effect on another (dependent). True experiments use random assignment.
    
3. **Quasi-experimental Research:** Similar to experimental research but lacks random assignment of participants to groups. Often used in social sciences where random assignment is impossible or unethical.
    
4. **Correlational Research:** Investigates the relationship between two or more variables without the researcher controlling or manipulating any of them. It shows associations, not causation.
    

#### B. Types of Qualitative Research

1. **Case Study:** An in-depth, detailed examination of a single case (a person, group, event, or organization) within a real-world context.
    
2. **Ethnographic Method:** Involves immersing oneself in a specific culture or community to understand their customs, behaviors, and interactions from the "inside."
    
3. **Phenomenological Method:** Focuses on the subjective lived experiences of individuals regarding a specific phenomenon (e.g., "What is it like to survive a natural disaster?").
    
4. **Narrative Model:** Relies on the written or spoken words or visual representation of individuals. It focuses on the stories people tell to understand their lives and the world.
    
5. **Historical Model:** Systematically examines past events to give an account of what happened in the past. It involves interpreting historical data to understand current issues.
    
6. **Grounded Theory Method:** A method where the researcher collects data first and then develops a theory _from_ (grounded in) that data, rather than starting with a hypothesis.
