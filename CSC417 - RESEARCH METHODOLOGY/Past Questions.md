1. **Basic (Fundamental/Pure) Research:** This is research driven by curiosity or a desire to expand knowledge in a specific scientific area. **(a) List any six (6) headings of a research proposal.**

1. Introduction / Background of Study
    
2. Problem Statement
    
3. Aim and Objectives
    
4. Literature Review
    
5. Methodology
    
6. Expected Outcomes / Contribution to Knowledge _(Others: Scope/Limitations, Project Timeline/Gantt Chart, References)._
    

**(b) Discuss the contents of each heading in (a).**

1. **Introduction:** Introduces the general area of study, provides context, and briefly outlines the research focus.
    
2. **Problem Statement:** Clearly defines the specific issue or gap in knowledge that the research intends to address. It explains _why_ the research is necessary.
    
3. **Aim and Objectives:** The _Aim_ is the broad goal. The _Objectives_ are the specific, measurable steps taken to achieve that goal.
    
4. **Literature Review:** Summarizes existing research to show what has been done and how the proposed project fits into the current body of knowledge.
    
5. **Methodology:** Describes the tools, techniques, algorithms, or data collection methods that will be used to carry out the research.
    
6. **Expected Outcomes:** Describes what the researcher anticipates finding or creating (e.g., a new software application, a framework, or statistical data).

### **QUESTION 4**

**(a) Differentiate between the following concepts in research:**

**(i) Dependent and Independent Variables:**

- **Independent Variable:** This is the variable that is changed or controlled in a scientific experiment to test the effects on the dependent variable. It is the "cause."
    
- **Dependent Variable:** This is the variable being tested and measured in an experiment. It changes based on the independent variable. It is the "effect."
    

**(ii) Qualitative and Quantitative Research:**

- **Quantitative Research:** Focuses on gathering numerical data and generalizing it across groups of people or to explain a particular phenomenon. It uses statistical, mathematical, and computational techniques.
    
- **Qualitative Research:** Focuses on obtaining data through open-ended and conversational communication. It is used to gain an understanding of underlying reasons, opinions, and motivations. It analyzes words and meanings rather than numbers.
    

**(b) Using the paper in Appendix One (1), answer the following:**

**(i) What do you understand by precision and Recall?** Based on the formulas in the paper (Equations 1 & 2):

- **Precision** is the ratio of true positive matches to the total number of items identified as positive (True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives)). It measures how accurate the positive predictions were.
    
- **Recall** is the ratio of true positive matches to the total number of actual positive items existing in the dataset (True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives)). It measures how well the system found _all_ the duplicates.
    

**(ii) Was the research qualitative, quantitative or mixed?** The research is **Quantitative**. _Reason:_ It deals with numerical data sets (31,177 records), uses algorithms and mathematical logic (Probability/Blocking), and evaluates performance using statistical metrics (Precision and Recall percentages).

### **QUESTION 5**

**(a) Define Research Philosophy.** Research philosophy refers to the set of beliefs concerning the nature of the reality being investigated (ontology) and the nature of knowledge (epistemology). It outlines the researcher's worldview and justifies the way data is gathered and analyzed.

**(b) Discuss the three (3) types of Research Philosophy.**

1. **Positivism:** Believes that reality is independent of social actors and that knowledge should be based on objective, observable, and measurable facts. It typically uses quantitative methods and scientific experimentation to test hypotheses.
    
2. **Interpretivism:** Believes that reality is socially constructed and subjective. It focuses on understanding the meanings humans attach to their actions. It typically uses qualitative methods like interviews and observation.
    
3. **Pragmatism:** Argues that the most important determinant of the research philosophy adopted is the research question. It focuses on "what works" and practical solutions, often combining both positivist and interpretivist approaches (mixed methods).
    

**(c) What philosophy did the research in Appendix one (1) adopted?** The research adopted a **Positivist** philosophy. _Reason:_ The study treats the data as objective facts, applies a scientific method (algorithm) to test a hypothesis, and relies on statistical measurement (accuracy, precision, recall) to validate the truth.

### **QUESTION 6**

**(a) List any five (5) ingredients of Research Methodology.**

1. Research Design
    
2. Population and Sampling
    
3. Data Collection Methods/Instruments
    
4. Data Analysis Procedures
    
5. Ethical Considerations _(Others: Validity and Reliability)._
    

**(b) Discuss the five (5) steps involved in a research process.**

1. **Identifying the Research Problem:** The researcher locates a gap in knowledge or a specific problem that needs solving.
    
2. **Reviewing the Literature:** The researcher studies existing work to understand the background and avoid duplication.
    
3. **Formulating Hypotheses/Methodology:** The researcher predicts the relationship between variables or designs a system/method to solve the problem.
    
4. **Data Collection/Experimentation:** The researcher gathers the necessary data through surveys, experiments, or system implementation.
    
5. **Data Analysis and Conclusion:** The collected data is processed to determine if the hypothesis was correct or if the system works, leading to a conclusion.
    

**(c) Define the following terms:**

**(i) Research:** A systematic and organized effort to investigate a specific problem that needs a solution.

**(ii) Problem statement:** A concise description of an issue to be addressed or a condition to be improved upon. It identifies the gap between the current state and the desired state.

**(iii) Closed ended questions:** Questions in a survey or interview that limit the respondent to a set of pre-defined answers (e.g., Yes/No, Multiple Choice).

**(iv) Open ended questions:** Questions that allow respondents to answer in their own words, providing detailed and qualitative information.

**(v) Research questions:** The specific questions that the research study sets out to answer. They guide the methodology and analysis.
